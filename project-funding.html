Small-Business Financing

Client qualification and working-capital discussions during my finance internship at High Rise Capital.

On this pageThe contextMy approachFinancial relevanceSkills developed

The context

During my finance internship at High Rise Capital, I contacted small-business owners, qualified prospects, and identified working-capital needs. I asked about their priorities before explaining potential financing options.

My approach

I discussed merchant cash advances, lines of credit, and business loans with owners seeking $25,000–$100,000 in financing. I clarified product structures and repayment terms and connected funding discussions to needs such as payroll and advertising.

  • Conducted 100+ daily outbound contacts to identify and qualify prospective business clients.
  • Identified business funding needs, explained how working capital could cover payroll or advertising, and connected financing options to those priorities.
  • Clarified funding structures and repayment terms in plain language.
  • Maintained prospect records and helped advance qualified leads through application and funding stages.

Financial relevance

Working-capital discussions connect a business’s need for cash with the amount and repayment structure of potential financing. Explaining those terms clearly was central to the client conversations I supported.

I translated product terms into clear explanations and kept conversations focused on each business’s funding needs.

Skills developed

Through daily outreach, I practiced listening for business needs, explaining financing terms concisely, and organizing follow-up. These habits help me connect financial information to the decisions a business faces.
